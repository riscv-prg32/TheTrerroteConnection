# The tribute, originality and provenance

This file records intentions and process. It is **not a legal opinion**, and no formal clearance has been done.

## The tribute

This game is a homage to Tony Tammaro (Vincenzo Sarnelli, Naples, 1961), il Grande Maestro, a myth of many generations.
It says so on its first screen, on the title, on the pause screen, in the firmware's status band, and in the README,
metadata and colophon, and `tests/` check that it does.

It is **unofficial**: not made, endorsed or reviewed by him.

## What is taken from him

Only references, as any homage does: his name, the world of the tamarri he made famous, and the idea of the Ape Car
and of a Patrizia who waits. The "Trerrote" song and the phrase *la monnezza della gente* come from the project's
brief. The Italian Wikipedia page and tonytammaro.com, consulted while writing this, confirm "Patrizia" as his
best-known song but don't mention "Trerrote", so that title has not been independently verified.

## What is not taken from him

- **No recording, sample, lyric or image.** No text in the game comes from a song.
- **No melody.** The score was composed without reference to any recording or transcription of his songs: they were
  neither heard nor read in making it. It shares with them only the conventions of a genre.

An honest limit: "composed without reference" is a statement of process, not a similarity check. Short tonal phrases in
a shared idiom can coincide by chance, and nobody has compared the score with his catalogue. Someone who knows those
songs well should listen before release.

## Worth considering before publishing

1. **Asking him.** The most useful single step, and the courteous one. A living artist's name is on the box; a tribute is
   much better with a yes. Rules on the use of a name differ from those on copyright, and this file does not judge them.
2. **"Ape"** is a Piaggio trademark. The game uses the word descriptively, and its sprite is a generic three-wheeler
   with no badge. The in-game text says *trerrote*.
3. **"Mozzarella di Bufala"** on the truck is a protected designation used as scenery. The truck is fictional, with
   no brand.
4. **Real places** are shown in good humour. The holes in the road are a joke.
